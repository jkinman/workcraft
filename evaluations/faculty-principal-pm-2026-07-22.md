## Block F: Interview Prep — STAR+R Stories

> For use if a screen is secured. Maps to Faculty's stated requirements.

---

### Story 1: Delivering AI products that solve tangible business problems
**Requirement:** "Hands-on experience delivering complex data, analytics, or AI-powered products"

**S:** Highspot, a sales enablement SaaS platform, needed to add LLM-based features to help enterprise reps find and use content faster.
**T:** I was tasked with designing and shipping the AI integration layer — scoping what models to use, how to present results, and how to validate with real users.
**A:** I led the technical discovery, defined the product spec with the PM team, implemented the Ruby/React feature in production, instrumented it for quality measurement, and iterated based on user feedback.
**R:** Feature shipped to production, adopted by enterprise customers, demonstrable improvement in content retrieval efficiency.
**Reflection:** What I'd do differently: build the eval framework before writing code — we discovered hallucination edge cases late. This is why product and data science need to co-design from day 0.

---

### Story 2: Consulting acumen — guiding non-technical executives
**Requirement:** "Strong consulting acumen and stakeholder management skills"

**S:** At Angry Mob, a client (mid-market business) wanted to "use AI" without a clear understanding of what that meant or what problem to solve.
**T:** Help them move from vague AI ambition to a concrete, fundable product roadmap.
**A:** Ran a discovery sprint: stakeholder interviews, problem mapping, capability audit, build vs. buy analysis. Produced a prioritized AI opportunity map with ROI framing for each initiative.
**R:** Client had a clear 3-initiative roadmap, eliminated 2 shiny-object projects, and proceeded with one scoped MVP — saving estimated 6 months of wasted engineering spend.
**Reflection:** The hardest part was managing the executive who wanted the AI to "do everything." Anchoring to business outcome (not technology capability) was the key move.

---

### Story 3: Entrepreneurial mindset / 0→1 product building
**Requirement:** "Entrepreneurial mindset... building capabilities from the ground up"

**S:** SwimOS was a 0→1 platform for real-time competitive intelligence with no existing playbook.
**T:** As co-founder/engineer, I wore product, engineering, and GTM hats simultaneously.
**A:** Ran customer discovery, defined MVP scope, built the real-time data pipeline architecture, designed the UX, and managed early customer conversations.
**R:** Built a working platform, established early customer relationships, demonstrated PMF signal sufficient to continue development.
**Reflection:** The biggest lesson: "product instinct" isn't a job title — it's the habit of asking "who does this help and how do we know it worked?" before writing any code.

---

### Story 4: Agile, pragmatic problem-solving
**Requirement:** "Agile, pragmatic approach to problem-solving, focused on getting things done"

**S:** Building Hermes Agent, I needed to integrate 15+ external APIs (web search, Fusion 360 CAD, MCP servers, TTS, email) into a coherent agentic system.
**T:** Design an architecture that's extensible without becoming a spaghetti integration nightmare.
**A:** Defined a skill-based plugin model, wrote the integration layer, tested iteratively with real use cases, shipped and iterated weekly.
**R:** A working, production-grade personal AI agent system used daily — with modular architecture that allows new tools without breaking existing ones.
**Reflection:** The pragmatic key was "ship it working at 80%, document the 20%, iterate in the field." Perfect architecture upfront would have meant never shipping.

## Recommendation

**Skip (unless relocation to UK is on the table).**

The role itself is genuinely interesting and technically well-suited to Joel's AI depth. The company is legitimate and respected. But three structural barriers make this a low-ROI application:

1. **London-hybrid is not negotiable.** Faculty's entire workforce appears UK-based. There is no evidence of remote-from-Canada hiring. This isn't a "ask and they might say yes" situation — it's a hiring infrastructure barrier (payroll entity, benefits, compliance).

2. **No PM title on the CV.** The JD explicitly asks for "a solid track record in product management leadership." Joel will be screened against people with that track record at the first filter. Without a referral or a very compelling narrative, this is likely a no-pass at the ATS stage.

3. **Comp doesn't improve at relocation.** Even if Joel were open to moving to London, £90K–£115K in London costs more to live on than $150K CAD in Vancouver. The effective comp degrades.

**If Joel wants to keep this as a market intelligence reference:** Faculty is worth watching. As agentic AI consulting grows, they may open remote/distributed roles. They're the kind of firm that would value Joel's exact profile at a slightly different point in his career arc (after 1–2 years of explicit PM scope, even in a consulting context).

**Hedge play:** If Joel's Highspot/Seismic transition frees up 6 months, a focused "get PM title" effort via Angry Mob (call it "Head of Product" on consulting engagements) would make this exact archetype of role much more accessible in 12–18 months.
