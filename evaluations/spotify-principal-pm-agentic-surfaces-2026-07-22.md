### Story 1: Agentic Product Ownership (Hermes Agent)
**Situation:** I wanted a personal AI assistant that could orchestrate multi-agent workflows — not just answer questions but take actions across systems.
**Task:** Design and build an agentic framework from scratch, defining what agents should do autonomously vs. when to involve the user (HITL).
**Action:** Architected Hermes with tool-calling, memory, skills system, multi-provider routing, and sub-agent delegation patterns. Made explicit product decisions: what should be transparent to the user, what should be deferred, when to ask vs. act.
**Result:** Daily-use production system with 50+ integrated tools, persistent memory, cross-session context. Became my primary working environment.
**Reflection:** What I learned about agentic UX: users don't want agents that do everything autonomously — they want agents that are *transparent about their decisions*. That's the eval bar: not "did the agent succeed" but "does the user trust what it did."

*Why this matters for Spotify:* Directly maps to "establish the evaluation bar for agentic experiences" and "decisive under incomplete information."

### Story 2: LLM Features in Production (Highspot)
**Situation:** Highspot wanted to integrate LLMs into their sales enablement platform. No internal playbook existed. Everyone had opinions, few had shipped anything.
**Task:** Own the technical direction AND product definition for LLM-powered features — evaluating what was feasible, what was safe to ship to enterprise customers, and what needed more time.
**Action:** Defined the capability assessment process, drove architecture decisions (retrieval-augmented vs. fine-tuned), shipped features in production Ruby/React with real enterprise users, built internal guardrails for LLM output reliability.
**Result:** Multiple LLM features in production, adopted by enterprise customers.
**Reflection:** The hardest call was *what not to ship* — recommending we kill a feature that was technically impressive but unreliable at scale. That's the decision that builds credibility with skeptics.

*Why this matters for Spotify:* Maps directly to "exercise clear, traceable judgment" and "balance investment across maturing and emerging technologies."

### Story 3: Strategic Deprecation / Technology Transition
**Situation:** [At Highspot or consulting] An existing system was working but a new approach (LLM-based) was clearly better for certain use cases, while the old system was more reliable for others.
**Task:** Make the call on where to maintain the old stack vs. transition to the new — and communicate that decision clearly to stakeholders who had emotional investment in the old system.
**Action:** Built evaluation framework comparing old vs. new on specific dimensions (reliability, coverage, user satisfaction, latency). Made explicit the criteria for "good enough to replace" vs. "needs more work." Got buy-in by showing the framework, not just the conclusion.
**Result:** Clear migration plan adopted. Stakeholders who were skeptical became advocates because they understood the decision logic.
**Reflection:** "The AI recommended it" is not a decision. The PM's job is to own the decision the AI informed.

*Why this matters for Spotify:* Directly mirrors the JD's language about "where the established recommendation stack remains durable vs. where to deprecate in favor of agents and the Large Personalization Model."

### Story 4: Prototyping Over Docs
**Situation:** A client engagement at Angry Mob where the brief was vague and stakeholders had different assumptions about what "agentic AI" meant.
**Task:** Align a cross-functional team on what to build before committing to a roadmap.
